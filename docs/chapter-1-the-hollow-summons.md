# Chapter 1 — The Hollow Summons

- Status: Working story and greybox design
- Scope: First complete chapter and collaboration test
- Provisional critical-path playtime: 45 minutes
- Canon status: Place functions and magical rules are working commitments; personal names and final creature art remain provisional

## Chapter premise

Two witches enter the abandoned magical settlement of Moonhollow to find their missing mentor and restore the failing ward that protects nearby villages. A corrupted guardian and the smaller creatures it creates have overrun the first Sentinel Tower. After containing the creatures and restoring the tower, the witches learn that their mentor entered the deeper maze voluntarily because someone is sabotaging Moonhollow from within the High Coven's own network.

## World and location definitions

Most witches and wizards live throughout the ordinary world in villages, cities, family estates, cottages, and private towers. A coven is an alliance, not necessarily a shared household. The High Coven maintains a fortified meeting place outside Moonhollow where witches train, exchange knowledge, prepare expeditions, and gather during emergencies.

### The Coven House

The inhabited stronghold overlooking the Moonhollow valley. It is the recurring campaign hub and the place where the playable witches meet the Coven Leader, prepare, trade, develop relationships, and return after expeditions.

### Moonhollow

An abandoned magical settlement inside a secluded valley. It once contained homes, gardens, libraries, workshops, and places of magical study. Its beautiful gardens also served as layered defenses. After a magical disaster a generation ago, the High Coven evacuated Moonhollow and raised a ward around it. The public explanation for the disaster is incomplete.

### The Moon Gate

The only known stable entrance through the boundary ward. Passing through it marks the transition from the inhabited world into Moonhollow. A failed local tower can make the gate or the route beyond it unstable, but the gate is not itself a moon-seal.

### The Garden Maze

Moonhollow's overgrown outer district. Its walls, roots, fountains, and paths were designed to move in controlled ways. With the nearest Sentinel Tower distorted, the maze shifts unpredictably and has begun growing beyond its old boundary.

### The Sunken Court

A broad, partially flooded courtyard between the Garden Maze and the first Sentinel Tower. Broken fountains, raised walks, ruined pillars, and containment circles make it a readable combat arena. It is the site of the Briarheart encounter.

### The West Sentinel Tower

The first tower reached in Chapter 1 and the missing Keeper's post. Its failure has destabilized the Garden Maze and opened a local tear in the boundary ward.

## Sentinel Towers, moon-seals, wards, and Rifts

These terms describe one connected system:

```text
moonlight + attuned witchcraft
             ↓
      fixed moon-seal lens
             ↓
       Sentinel Tower
        ↙     ↓      ↘
 warning   stable    local section
 signal    pathways  of boundary ward
```

### Sentinel Tower

A Sentinel Tower is part watchtower, part lighthouse, and part magical stabilizer. Each tower serves one district of Moonhollow and has three understandable jobs:

1. Detect severe magical disturbances and send a warning to the Coven House.
2. Keep nearby defensive paths and structures in their intended configuration.
3. Project one connected section of the ward surrounding Moonhollow.

The ring of towers works together. Failure of one tower does not instantly destroy the entire ward, but it creates a weak local section and forces neighboring towers to carry more strain.

### Moon-seal

A moon-seal is the large, circular moonstone lens permanently mounted inside a Sentinel Tower. It is not a collectible key, a loose fragment, or an object the witches normally carry. It receives lunar energy, responds to attuned witchcraft, and lets the tower shape that energy into a stable ward.

A seal has three readable states:

- **Lit:** Correctly aligned. The tower broadcasts normally and nearby paths remain stable.
- **Distorted:** Corruption or false instructions have reversed part of its pattern. The tower flickers, paths shift, and creatures can be affected by the leaking magic.
- **Dark:** The seal has shut down. Its section of the ward is open and the district is no longer stabilized.

Restoring a distorted seal requires the witches to remove the source of corruption, align its physical rings, and attune it with magic. Two witches can hold complementary channels simultaneously. A lone witch can charge them sequentially, but the process is slower and attracts more danger.

### Ward and Rift

The ward is the connected magical boundary projected by the Sentinel Towers. A Rift is a visible tear in one local section of that ward; it is not yet established as a portal to another universe. The early story should preserve the question of whether apparent invaders came through the Rift or were already living inside Moonhollow.

When the West Tower is restored in Chapter 1, players see immediate proof that the system works: the tower lights, its warning beam resumes, the local Rift closes, the Garden Maze settles into stable paths, and the route home opens.

## Principal characters and objectives

Final personal names remain open. The roles below are the current character foundations.

### Purple Witch — storm magic

- **Backstory:** Her power once surged out of control. The missing Keeper defended her when others wanted her removed from training.
- **External objective:** Find the Keeper and protect the threatened settlements.
- **Internal objective:** Prove that her power can protect people rather than endanger them.
- **Encounter strength:** Direct pressure, interruption, and protection during dangerous channels.

### Green Witch — plant magic

- **Backstory:** She grew up among the farms now threatened by Moonhollow. The missing Keeper taught her that damaged living magic can sometimes be healed.
- **External objective:** Stop the maze's spread and protect her displaced community.
- **Internal objective:** Discover whether Moonhollow can be restored instead of merely destroyed or resealed.
- **Encounter strength:** Restraint, battlefield control, recovery, and faster setup for containment.

### Coven Leader

- **Backstory:** She and the missing Keeper were once close friends and explored Moonhollow together before it was abandoned.
- **Public objective:** Restore the West Tower before the local Rift reaches the villages.
- **Private complication:** She received more of the Keeper's final warning than she initially shares.
- **Story function:** A credible authority whose secrecy creates suspicion without proving guilt.

### Missing Keeper

- **Backstory:** Guardian of the West Tower and former teacher of both playable witches.
- **Objective:** Identify who issued a false command through the Sentinel network and prevent further sabotage.
- **Apparent fate:** The tower failed and a monster took or killed her.
- **Chapter 1 truth:** She left the tower voluntarily and followed evidence into the deeper maze.

### Hidden antagonist

The antagonist's identity remains concealed, but their Chapter 1 action and objective must be known to the designers. They sent a false command through an authorized Coven channel, distorted the West Tower's moon-seal, and corrupted its guardian. They want capable witches to reopen stable routes through Moonhollow that the antagonist cannot safely traverse alone.

## Major encounter — Briarheart and the Briarlings

### Creature concept

Briarheart is a large guardian shaped like a stag and formed from bark, pale stone, roots, and moonlit antlers. It was created to protect the Garden Maze and did not cross through the Rift. A black splinter of foreign magic lodged near its heart has distorted its protective instincts.

Briarheart can plant pieces of its corrupted body in the arena. These pieces rapidly grow into smaller **Briarlings**: aggressive root-creatures that harry the witches and channel protective magic back to Briarheart.

### Encounter rule

The encounter alternates between pressure on Briarheart and containment of its spawned Briarlings:

1. Briarheart begins exposed and uses charges, antler sweeps, thorn lines, and a clearly telegraphed moonburst.
2. At authored corruption or health thresholds, its antlers flare and seed pods appear at visible locations before a finite wave of Briarlings emerges.
3. Active Briarlings shield or slowly restore Briarheart. Continuing to attack only the boss becomes inefficient.
4. Every active Briarling must be weakened into a staggered state and secured in a containment circle. An unattended staggered Briarling eventually recovers.
5. Contained Briarlings remain visibly sealed and do not respawn. When the wave is contained, Briarheart's corruption is exposed for another coordinated damage window.
6. Later waves add positioning pressure, but the number of simultaneous enemies remains capped for camera clarity, fairness, and performance.
7. After the last wave, the black splinter is fully exposed. The witches can destroy the guardian or remove the corruption; both complete the required route, while cleansing it preserves the stronger story and relationship outcome.

### Collaboration and solo viability

- Green can restrain Briarlings and hold them staggered while Purple protects the channel or interrupts Briarheart.
- Purple can pressure clustered Briarlings and create safe containment time while Green restores injured allies.
- Both witches retain a baseline weaken-and-contain interaction so neither is useless alone.
- A solo player receives fewer simultaneous Briarlings and longer boss recovery windows. The mechanics remain the same rather than becoming a separate story route.
- The first greybox version should use two finite waves with no more than three active Briarlings at once. Counts can increase only after readability and performance testing.

### Story evidence from the battle

Containing the Briarlings reveals that they are fragments of Briarheart rather than an invading species. Removing the black splinter shows that the corruption carries an authenticated High Coven command pattern. This contradicts the briefing's assumption that every creature crossed through the Rift and points the investigation toward deliberate sabotage.

## Chapter 1 playable sequence

1. **Coven House:** Establish the two witches, their relationship with the missing Keeper, and the danger to the nearby settlements.
2. **Briefing:** Receive the Keeper's interrupted warning and the clear objective: reach the West Tower, find her, and restore its distorted moon-seal.
3. **Moon Gate:** Enter Moonhollow and learn that the unstable tower is causing the Garden Maze to shift.
4. **Garden Maze:** Traverse the outer paths, learn the containment-circle language, and find evidence that the Keeper reached the tower alive.
5. **Sunken Court:** Fight Briarheart while containing every spawned Briarling. Recover or examine the black corruption splinter.
6. **West Tower:** Discover that the Keeper left voluntarily after receiving a false command through an authorized Coven channel.
7. **Seal restoration:** Align and attune the fixed moon-seal. Collaboration performs the channels together; solo play completes them sequentially under greater pressure.
8. **Visible resolution:** The tower relights, the local Rift closes, the maze settles, and the exit route stabilizes.
9. **Return:** The witches leave through the stabilized route and bring the corruption evidence back to the Coven House.
10. **Chapter hook:** A final message confirms that the Keeper followed the saboteur's trail into the deeper maze and does not know whom inside the Coven she can trust.
